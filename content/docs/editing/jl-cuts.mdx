# J-Cut & L-Cut

## Introduction

In a **straight cut**, picture and sound change together. A **J-cut** lets the *incoming* sound arrive before the picture. An **L-cut** lets the *outgoing* sound linger under the new picture. Named for the shape those splits make on a timeline.

## Why it matters

- Dialogue and ambience can pull attention before the eye arrives.
- Smoothens scene changes without a dissolve.
- On the web: prefetch audio / voiceover ahead of a visual chapter, or keep a bed under a UI swap.

## Visual explanation

```text
J-cut:   picture  AAAAAAAAAAAAA|BBBBBBB

         sound    AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAA|BBBB
```

## Parameters

| Control | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Mode | J / L / straight |
| Audio offset | How far sound leads or trails the picture cut |

## Practical examples

1. **Chapter VO** — J-cut: hear the next narrator line before the hero swaps.
2. **Gallery** — L-cut: keep ambient bed under a hard image change.
3. **Game cutscenes** — lead with impact SFX before the camera angle changes.

## Implementation

Track two cut times:

```js
const pictureCut = 0.5;
const soundCut = pictureCut - offset; // J
// const soundCut = pictureCut + offset; // L
```

Drive picture visibility from `pictureCut`, audio source from `soundCut`.

## Performance notes

- Decode / unlock audio early if the J-cut must fire on first gesture.
- Don’t animate gain and picture on conflicting clocks without a shared timeline.

## Common mistakes

- Huge offsets that feel like a mistake, not a lead-in
- J-cutting into silence (no “B” audio to lead with)
- Forgetting captions when sound leads picture

## When to use / avoid

**Use** to glue scenes emotionally or to foreshadow.

**Avoid** when the new picture must land with a synchronized hit (logo sting, UI confirm).

## Key takeaways

- Picture and sound are independent edit axes.
- J leads with B sound; L trails A sound.
- Small offsets often feel more expensive than big ones.
