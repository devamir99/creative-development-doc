# Shot Types & Camera Angles

## See it first

<ShotTypesPlayground />

Drag framing size and switch angle. Notice: wide explains *where*, medium explains *what is happening*, close-up explains *what it feels like*.

## Introduction

A **shot type** describes how much of the subject and environment is visible. A **camera angle** describes the vertical relationship between camera and subject. Together they are the fastest visual grammar in cinema and 3D web scenes.

## Why it matters

- A product shown only in close-up may look beautiful but users may not understand scale or context.
- A character filmed from below feels powerful; from above, exposed or judged.
- Web heroes, product configurators, and scroll stories all inherit this grammar even when the “camera” is CSS or WebGL.

## Shot-size reference

| Shot | What dominates | Best for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme wide | Place | World-building, scale, isolation | Subject may disappear |
| Wide | Subject + environment | Geography, entrances, full product | Less emotion |
| Medium wide | Body/action + some space | Movement, interaction | Can feel generic |
| Medium | Upper body/action | Dialogue, tutorials, product use | Limited context |
| Medium close-up | Face + gesture | Conversation, reaction | Background weakens |
| Close-up | Face/detail | Emotion, material, decision | No geography |
| Extreme close-up | Texture/symbol | Eyes, logo, mechanism, clue | Abstract if overused |

## Angle reference

| Angle | Viewer feeling | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| Low angle | Power, monument, threat | Hero product, villain, architecture |
| Eye level | Neutral, honest | Dialogue, documentation, UI 3D |
| High angle | Vulnerability, overview | Maps, weakness, surveillance |
| Dutch/canted | Instability | Anxiety, stylized action — use sparingly |
| POV | “I am there” | Games, tutorials, immersive product |

## Visual explanation

Shot size answers **how close are we emotionally?** Angle answers **who has power?** A close-up from below is a powerful intimate subject. A wide shot from above can make the same subject feel lost.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| Distance / crop | Controls shot size |
| Camera height | Controls angle and dominance |
| Focal length | Changes compression; do not confuse with distance |
| Subject placement | Determines how much environment remains readable |

## Practical applications

1. **Product landing:** wide for context, medium for interaction, close-up for material.
2. **Portfolio case:** establishing wide, medium process, close-up outcome.
3. **Tutorial:** medium for hands/action, close-up only at the critical detail.
4. **Game/cinematic UI:** low angle for boss/hero, high angle for tactical overview.

## Implementation

In R3F, shot size is usually camera distance plus framing; angle is camera height relative to the look-at point.

```tsx
<PerspectiveCamera position={[0, cameraHeight, distance]} fov={40} />
```

Keep FOV fixed while learning. Otherwise you cannot tell whether the change came from lens or position.

## Common mistakes

- Calling every FOV change a “close-up”
- Using low angles for everything until nothing feels powerful
- Cutting from extreme wide to extreme close-up without story motivation
- Placing the camera high but aiming it like an eye-level shot, creating accidental surveillance feelings

## Performance & accessibility

Camera angle itself is cheap. Avoid constant reframing that causes motion discomfort. In interactive scenes, provide reset and respect reduced-motion settings.

## When to use / avoid

Use wide shots to orient, medium shots to explain action, close-ups to create feeling. Avoid close-ups when the user still needs spatial context.

## Key takeaways

- Shot size = emotional distance.
- Angle = power relationship.
- Change one variable at a time while learning.
- A sequence usually moves between geography, action, and emotion.
