# Dolly vs Zoom

## Introduction

**Dolly** moves the camera through space (here: along Z toward the subject).  
**Zoom** changes field of view while the eye stays put.

Both can make a subject occupy more of the frame. They are **not** the same operation. Dolly changes **spatial relationships** (parallax between near and far). Zoom changes the **optical cone** without relocating the eye.

## Why it matters

- Hitchcock / “vertigo” shots combine both on purpose; beginners often zoom when they meant to dolly (or the reverse).
- Product and architectural web 3D: faking a push-in with FOV alone usually lacks parallax and feels flat.
- Teaching clarity: once you have seen matched framing side by side, you stop equating the two.

## Visual explanation

The playground keeps the yellow subject at roughly the **same size** in both panels using:

```text
z × tan(fov / 2) ≈ constant
```

| Panel | What changes | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dolly** | Camera `z` closer · FOV fixed | Pillars and far blocks shift relative to the subject (parallax) |
| **Zoom** | FOV narrower · Camera `z` fixed | Subject grows optically; depth stack compresses without the eye moving |

At push amount `0` both sides match the baseline (`z=6`, `fov=40°`).

## Practical examples

1. **Product hero** — prefer a short dolly (or orbit) for tangible space; reserve FOV animation for optical stylization.
2. **Horror / unease** — dolly-zoom (both at once) is a separate effect; learn the two ingredients first.
3. **UI embedded 3D** — zoom-only “push” is cheaper but reads as a crop, not a move.

## Common mistakes

- Scaling the subject mesh to fake a dolly
- Animating FOV and calling it a camera move
- Comparing dolly vs zoom without matching subject size (you then confuse size change with technique)

## When to use / avoid

**Dolly** when space and parallax matter.  
**Zoom** when you want optical compression/expansion with a locked eye.  
**Both together** only when you intentionally want the vertigo counter-move.

## Key takeaways

- Same subject size ≠ same camera craft.
- Dolly = spatial; Zoom = optical.
- Matched framing reveals the real difference in the background.
