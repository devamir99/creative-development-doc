# Camera Movement

## See it first

<CameraMovementPlayground />

The top map shows the physical camera. The lower strip shows the resulting frame. The central distinction is simple: **pan/tilt rotate the eye; dolly/truck/orbit move the eye.**

## Introduction

Camera movement changes the viewer’s relationship to space over time. A moving camera can reveal, follow, pursue, inspect, or destabilize. A locked camera can create tension by refusing to move.

## Movement vocabulary

| Move | Camera does | Frame result | Typical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pan | Rotates left/right | World sweeps horizontally | Follow, survey, reveal |
| Tilt | Rotates up/down | World sweeps vertically | Scale, discovery |
| Dolly | Moves forward/back | Parallax changes, subject grows/shrinks | Approach, retreat, intimacy |
| Truck | Moves left/right | Background shifts against subject | Travel, parallel action |
| Pedestal | Moves up/down | Height changes without tilt alone | Reveal over obstacles |
| Orbit/arc | Curves around subject | Background rotates behind subject | Inspection, spectacle |
| Tracking | Follows subject | Subject stable, world moves | Journey, pursuit |
| Zoom | Changes FOV only | Size changes without spatial travel | Surveillance, shock — not a dolly |
| Handheld | Adds irregular motion | Imperfect human presence | Documentary, urgency |

## Why it matters

Movement is not decoration. It controls information timing: what is revealed now, what remains hidden, and how the body of the viewer feels. On the web, scroll often becomes the motor for camera movement.

## Visual explanation

If foreground and background shift relative to each other, the camera moved through space. If they scale together with little relative shift, you probably changed FOV/zoom.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Role |
|---|---|
| Path | Straight, arc, spline, handheld noise |
| Duration/easing | Emotional speed of travel |
| Look-at target | Fixed subject, moving subject, or free orientation |
| Start/end framing | Movement must begin and land on readable compositions |
| Stabilization | Smooth, mechanical, or intentionally shaky |

## Practical applications

1. **Product reveal:** slow dolly or orbit; land on a clean hero frame.
2. **Scroll story:** camera trucks through chapters as scroll progresses.
3. **Tutorial:** tracking keeps the working area centered while context moves.
4. **Cinematic tension:** locked camera while action approaches it.

## Implementation

Animate camera position and call `lookAt` (or use a target). Keep the path and look-at as separate concepts.

```ts
camera.position.lerpVectors(start, end, progress);
camera.lookAt(target);
```

For scroll, map normalized scroll progress to the same `progress` value.

## Common mistakes

- Moving every axis at once with no motivation
- Starting/ending on weak compositions
- Confusing zoom with dolly
- Endless orbit on content users need to read
- Handheld noise applied to precise UI or product inspection

## Performance & accessibility

Camera motion can trigger discomfort. Offer reduced-motion alternatives, avoid rapid rotation, and never make essential content readable only while moving.

## When to use / avoid

Use movement to reveal, follow, or change emotional distance. Avoid it when the frame already communicates clearly or users are performing precise tasks.

## Key takeaways

- Rotation and translation are different tools.
- Parallax proves spatial movement.
- Every move needs a start frame, journey, and landing frame.
- Motion should reveal information, not merely prove technology.
