# Field of View (FOV)

## Introduction

**Field of view (FOV)** is how wide the camera’s cone of vision is, usually measured in **degrees** for a perspective camera (vertical FOV in Three.js / R3F).

With the camera **locked in place**, changing FOV reframes the world optically: wide FOV exaggerates perspective; narrow FOV compresses depth and feels more “telephoto.”

This is **not** the same as dollying closer or farther. Dolly moves the eye through space; FOV changes the lens. That pair gets its own demo later (`DEMO-CAM-DOLLY-ZOOM`).

## Why it matters

- Product heroes look distorted with careless ultra-wide FOV.
- Narrow FOV can feel premium and compressed — or claustrophobic if overused.
- Scroll / cinematic web often fakes a “push in” with FOV; trained eyes spot it next to a true spatial move.

## Visual explanation

Hold **position** and **look direction** constant. Only FOV changes.

| FOV (approx) | Feel |
| --- | --- |
| ~24–35° | Tighter, more compressed depth |
| ~40–50° | Familiar “normal” product / dialogue band |
| ~70–90° | Wide, stretched edges, dramatic perspective |

Watch the yellow subject vs the far pillars: wide FOV pulls more of the set into frame and bends spatial relationships; tele FOV crops and stacks depth.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `fov` | Vertical field of view in degrees (Three.js `PerspectiveCamera.fov`) |
| Camera position | Fixed at `[0, 1.25, 6]` in this lab |
| Near / far | Clip planes — unchanged here |

## Practical examples

1. **Furniture / sneakers hero** — start ~40°; avoid 90° unless distortion is the point.
2. **Architectural peek** — wider FOV to sell scale; watch verticals at the frame edge.
3. **Portrait-like product** — narrower FOV + careful distance (distance is a separate control — don’t fake it only with FOV).

## Implementation

```tsx
<PerspectiveCamera
  makeDefault
  position={[0, 1.25, 6]}
  fov={40}
/>
```

After changing `fov` at runtime, call `camera.updateProjectionMatrix()` (the playground does this when the slider moves).

## Performance notes

- Cap `dpr`; use `frameloop="demand"` and invalidate on FOV change.
- Don’t mount Canvas on text-only routes — lazy-load the scene module.
- Dispose is handled when the Canvas unmounts with the lesson.

## Common mistakes

- Using FOV animation as a substitute for a dolly without saying so
- Extreme wide FOV on reflective products → melting edges
- Forgetting `updateProjectionMatrix()` after mutating `camera.fov`

## When to use / avoid

**Change FOV when** you intentionally want optical widen/narrow with a locked eye.  
**Prefer dolly/truck when** you want the parallax of moving through space.

## Key takeaways

- FOV = lens width; position = eye placement — different tools.
- Fixed camera + FOV change is the clean way to learn the optical effect.
