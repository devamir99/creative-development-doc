# Center vs Off-Center

## Introduction

**Center** reads as statement, emblem, certainty. **Off-center** activates surrounding space and often needs **lead room** — empty space in the direction of gaze or motion.

## Why it matters

- Logos and icons often want center.
- Narrative frames often want off-center + lead room.
- Scroll can drift from center to off-center as the story opens up.

## Visual explanation

The arrow shows facing. Move the subject and watch lead room on the facing side shrink or grow.

## Practical examples

1. **App icon** — centered on a quiet field.
2. **Character still** — eyes left, lead room right.
3. **Scroll open** — start centered wordmark → drift to thirds as product enters.

## Implementation

Track facing direction as data; keep a minimum lead-room percentage when composing programmatically.

## Performance notes

- Layout only.

## Common mistakes

- Off-center without enough lead room (nose against the edge)
- Centering narrative action shots that need space to “breathe”
- Mirroring facing but forgetting to flip lead room

## When to use / avoid

**Use** center for emblems; off-center for living scenes.
**Avoid** accidental near-center that looks unfinished.

## Key takeaways

- Center = statement; off-center = activated space.
- Lead room follows facing / motion.
- Either choice should be intentional.
