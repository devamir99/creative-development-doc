# Negative Space

## See it first

<NegativeSpacePlayground />

Increase emptiness. The subject does not become less important by accident — isolation can make it more important, calmer, lonelier, or more expensive.

## Introduction

**Negative space** is the area around and between subjects. It is not leftover space. It shapes silhouette, directs attention, creates breathing room, and gives future motion somewhere to go.

## Why it matters

- Crowded frames compete; isolated subjects command.
- Empty space in the direction of gaze/movement creates anticipation.
- Premium brands often use space as confidence: nothing needs to shout.

## Types and effects

| Pattern | Feeling/use |
|---|---|
| Space around subject | Isolation, importance |
| Space ahead of movement/gaze | Anticipation, lead room |
| Space behind subject | Departure, loneliness, aftermath |
| Large sky/field | Scale, vulnerability, freedom |
| Tight negative space | Pressure, intimacy, tension |

## Visual explanation

Positive space is the object. Negative space is the shape around it. Strong compositions design both silhouettes.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| Subject scale | Smaller subject increases spatial dominance of emptiness |
| Position | Determines whether space feels like lead room or dead room |
| Contrast of field | Quiet fields calm; busy fields destroy negative space |
| Direction | Space should support gaze/motion vector |

## Practical applications

1. **Luxury hero:** tiny product, huge calm field.
2. **Horror:** subject compressed against edge with threatening empty space.
3. **UI empty state:** illustration isolated so the next action is clear.
4. **Scroll reveal:** reserve empty area that later receives text or product.

## Implementation

Design the empty area explicitly in layout tokens: min-height, padding, max-width, and subject scale. Do not treat whitespace as whatever remains after components are placed.

## Common mistakes

- Empty space with no relationship to subject direction
- Calling accidental leftover gaps “minimalism”
- Filling every gap with badges, gradients, and decorative shapes
- Too much isolation for dense tools where scanning matters

## When to use / avoid

Use for focus, luxury, anticipation, and emotional scale. Avoid when users need dense comparison or rapid data scanning.

## Key takeaways

- Negative space is designed, not empty.
- It can isolate, calm, threaten, or anticipate.
- Direction matters: lead room differs from dead room.
