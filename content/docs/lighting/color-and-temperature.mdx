# Color Harmony & Temperature

## See it first

<ColorTemperaturePlayground />

Warm light feels like sun, fire, skin, and intimacy. Cool light feels like night, technology, distance, or clinical space. Meaning depends on context and contrast.

## Introduction

**Color temperature** describes warm-to-cool appearance. In photography it is associated with Kelvin, but in web/3D teaching, the important skill is relative: what is warmer or cooler than its surroundings?

## Harmony systems

| System | Relationship | Feeling/use |
|---|---|---|
| Monochromatic | One hue, varied value/saturation | Calm, premium, focused |
| Analogous | Neighboring hues | Natural, gentle |
| Complementary | Opposite hues | Tension, pop, sport/energy |
| Split complementary | One hue + two neighbors of opposite | Balanced contrast |
| Triadic | Three spaced hues | Playful, systematic |
| Neutral + accent | Grays + one signal | Product/UI clarity |

## Value and saturation

Hue is only one dimension. **Value** (light/dark) creates hierarchy and readability. **Saturation** controls intensity and cheapness risk. A palette can fail even with fashionable hues if values are muddy.

## Tone mapping & exposure (concept)

Tone mapping compresses bright/dark range into displayable color. It affects contrast and saturation. In Three.js, renderer tone mapping and output color space must be treated as part of art direction, not default plumbing.

## Why it matters

Color directs attention, encodes brand, signals state, and creates time/place. Warm subject against cool background is one of the strongest separation tools.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| Key color temperature | Main mood |
| Fill contrast temperature | Warm/cool separation |
| Accent saturation | Attention strength |
| Background value | Subject separation |
| Exposure/tone mapping | Overall contrast and roll-off |

## Practical applications

1. **Night product:** cool environment, warm product light.
2. **Health/clinical:** cool neutrals, controlled saturation.
3. **Food:** warm key and careful saturation.
4. **Creative Lab itself:** near-black neutrals + yellow accent.

## Implementation

```css
:root {
  --accent: rgb(250 250 5);
  --cool-fill: #b9ccff;
  --warm-key: #ffd9a0;
}
```

In R3F, set light `color` and material colors deliberately; check under the actual tone mapping used by the scene.

## Common mistakes

- Using pure complementary colors at equal saturation everywhere
- Warm and cool lights mixed with no hierarchy
- Ignoring color-vision accessibility for meaning
- Judging palette colors isolated instead of in the final lit scene

## Accessibility

Never encode critical meaning by color alone. Check text contrast and avoid red/green-only state differences.

## When to use / avoid

Use warm/cool contrast for separation and time. Avoid high saturation across large areas unless the brand is intentionally loud.

## Key takeaways

- Temperature is relative.
- Value creates hierarchy before hue.
- One accent beats a rainbow in most product systems.
